# Myo-reps: evidence and product rules

Updated: 2026-07-28
Review type: focused scoping review

## Product protocol

Fitness AI uses the owner's requested default:

- one activation set close to technical failure;
- three mini-sets with the same load;
- each mini-set targets 30% of the activation-set repetitions, rounded to the
  nearest whole repetition with a minimum of one;
- 30 seconds between the activation set and mini-sets, and between mini-sets.

The activation and every mini-set are stored as ordinary `working` sets. Their
actual repetitions and load therefore contribute to history, volume-load,
muscle statistics and AI analysis without a separate accounting path.

## Evidence summary

The direct longitudinal evidence is promising but narrow. Bradshaw et al.
(2026) compared Myo-reps with three traditional straight sets over eight weeks
in 22 resistance-trained men using three chest exercises. Both groups improved
muscle size and strength, with no detected between-group differences, while the
Myo-reps group accumulated about 30% less volume-load. The accompanying acute
comparison (`n=9`) also found substantially shorter exercise duration.

This supports Myo-reps as a time-efficient alternative. It does not establish
that Myo-reps is superior: the sample was small, the intervention was short,
only men and chest training were studied, and a non-significant difference is
not proof of equivalence without an adequately powered equivalence or
non-inferiority design.

Broader reviews agree with the cautious interpretation:

- Jukic et al. (2021), 17 studies: alternative set structures had negligible
  average effects on strength and hypertrophy compared with traditional sets.
- Cowley et al. (2026), 62 randomized studies: strength and power were similar
  across advanced and traditional methods, and no method consistently produced
  greater hypertrophy.
- Refalo et al. (2023): momentary failure was not categorically superior for
  hypertrophy; proximity to failure should be treated as a dose, not a binary
  requirement.
- Vieira et al. (2022): failure training produces more acute fatigue, muscle
  damage and perceived effort, so recovery cost must be considered.

## Coaching rules

A high-quality Myo-reps block has:

- a genuinely hard activation set;
- the same load across activation and mini-sets;
- measured rests close to the planned 30 seconds;
- mini-set repetitions near 30% of the activation set;
- controlled technique and no pain signal.

A block deserves criticism when the activation set is clearly easy, the load
changes without a reason, measured rest is much longer than planned, or the
first mini-set immediately misses the target by a large margin. Missing RPE or
rest data is uncertainty, not evidence of bad work.

One activation plus three mini-sets is not described as four full traditional
sets one-for-one. The app counts all real work, while the trainer evaluates the
cluster by load, repetitions, rest and effort. Strength progression is judged
primarily from the activation set; expected repetition loss in mini-sets is not
regression.
